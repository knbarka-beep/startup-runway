# Data specification

All data is **synthetic**. Nordlys Analytics ApS is a fictional company. Customer names are random combinations of invented word parts; any resemblance to a real company is a coincidence.

Generated by `scripts/generate.py` with fixed seed 42. Monthly granularity, 24 months from Oct 2024 to Sep 2026. Files land in `data/raw/`.

## The company

- Copenhagen B2B SaaS selling analytics subscriptions to mid-sized Nordic companies.
- Cash in bank before the round: €350.000. Seed round of €3,0m received in Oct 2024 (month 1).
- 15 employees in Oct 2024, 25 in Sep 2026.
- Three plans (Starter, Growth, Enterprise), each sold on annual or monthly billing.

## Tables

| File | Columns | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `customers.csv` | customer_id, name, segment, plan, billing, start_month, end_month, mrr | `end_month` is the first month the customer no longer pays (empty if active). `mrr` is the MRR in the customer's last active month. 40 customers started before Oct 2024. |
| `mrr_movements.csv` | month, customer_id, type, amount | Types: new, expansion, contraction, churn. Contraction and churn are negative. Customers that started before Oct 2024 get one `new` row at their real start month with the MRR they had on 1 Oct 2024 (their earlier history is compressed). |
| `mrr_snapshot.csv` | month, customer_id, mrr | MRR of every active customer each month. Written by the simulation itself and used only to check that movements add up (see `scripts/reconcile.py`). |
| `headcount.csv` | employee_id, role, team, start_month, end_month, monthly_cost | Teams: engineering, sales, customer success, G&A. `end_month` is the first month the person is gone. `monthly_cost` is loaded cost (gross salary x 1,12). |
| `opex.csv` | month, category, amount | Categories: cloud hosting, software, marketing, office, professional fees, other. |
| `cash.csv` | month, opening_balance, collections, payments_payroll, payments_opex, payments, funding, closing_balance | `payments = payments_payroll + payments_opex`. Payroll and opex are split so the payroll check can work. |

## Simple accounting rules

- Revenue in a month = MRR that month. No part-month proration.
- Monthly plans pay their MRR every month.
- Annual plans pay 12 x MRR upfront in their start month and at every anniversary. So collections jump in renewal months and are low in between.
- Annual customers can only expand, shrink or leave at their anniversary. Monthly customers can do so any month.
- Salaries and costs are paid in the month they occur. No VAT, no corporate tax, no payables or receivables timing.

## The planted story

These events are deliberately built into the data so the investor updates have something real to talk about.

1. **Hiring push, spring 2026.** Six hires: Mar 2026 (2 sales, 1 engineering), Apr 2026 (1 sales, 1 engineering), May 2026 (1 customer success). Payroll jumps by about €38.000 a month. New customer intake only rises from Jul 2026, so burn goes up first.
2. **Largest customer churns, Jul 2026.** Customer C001 (Enterprise, annual, €9.400 MRR) does not renew at its anniversary. MRR drops by €9.400 that month.
3. **Price increase from Jun 2026.** New contracts signed from Jun 2026 are priced 10 % higher. Existing customers keep their price.
4. **Quiet good month, Nov 2025.** Expansion probability is raised for that month only: 9 customers expand, +€2.280 MRR, the strongest expansion month in the period.
5. **One-off cost, Apr 2026.** A recruiting agency fee of €24.000 booked under professional fees, linked to the hiring push.

Also in the data but not planted as a story: one account executive leaves in Jun 2025 and is replaced in Sep 2025, and random churn, expansion and contraction every month.

## End state (Sep 2026)

- Closing cash: €1.579.630
- Average net burn, last 3 months: about €110.300
- Runway at that burn: about 14,3 months

The parameters were set once to land in the brief's 10 to 16 month range and were not tuned to look dramatic.
